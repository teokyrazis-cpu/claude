3 The Framework: Data Creator vs Aggregator

This is the single most important concept for competitive positioning. Master this framework and you'll win more deals.

The Core Positioning

Citizen creates proprietary data. We have 20M+ users on the ground reporting in real-time, plus direct 911/CAD integrations. This data doesn't exist anywhere else.

Competitors aggregate existing data. They scrape Twitter, news feeds, and public sources—data you could access yourself, just slower.

This is our defensible moat. They can't replicate 20M users. We can't be commoditized by better aggregation algorithms.

"The difference between 90 seconds and 20 minutes isn't technology—it's whether you CREATE the data or wait for someone else to publish it. We create. They aggregate. That's the game."

How to Use This Framework

  • Early in discovery: "Are you currently creating proprietary safety data, or aggregating public sources?"
  • When they mention Everbridge/Dataminr/Samdesk: "Great tools for aggregation. Do you need data creation capabilities for faster detection?"
  • In proposals: "Our moat: proprietary data from 20M users + 911 integration. Can't be replicated through better scraping."
  • During pricing objections: "You're paying for proprietary data creation, not aggregated feeds you could access directly."

Use This Framing in EVERY Competitive Conversation

  • Position around the data source, not features
  • Emphasize "created" vs "aggregated" at least 2-3 times per call
  • Connect data creation to speed advantage (90 seconds vs 10-20 minutes)
  • Make it about their use case: "For your facilities, do you need to know NOW or in 15 minutes?"

4 Live Call Tactics

How to deploy competitive intelligence during conversations without sounding defensive or aggressive.

Don't

  • Bash competitors directly ("They're terrible at X")
  • Make claims you can't back up with data
  • Sound defensive when they mention competitor names
  • Interrupt when they're explaining their current setup
  • Use the battlecards as a script—use them as a guide

Do

  • Position around use cases and outcomes, not feature comparison
  • Ask discovery questions from the cards to understand their needs
  • Use the talk tracks verbatim (they're battle-tested with real customers)
  • Emphasize complementary positioning when appropriate (AlertMedia, Rave, Crisis24)
  • Lead with speed advantage: "90 seconds vs 10-20 minutes when it matters most"
  • Acknowledge competitor strengths before pivoting to differentiation

The Acknowledge-Pivot Framework

  • Acknowledge: "Everbridge is a strong enterprise platform with great global coverage."
  • Pivot: "The question is whether your use case requires real-time proprietary data or aggregated feeds with 15-20 minute lag."
  • Discovery: "Walk me through what happens when there's an active shooter near one of your facilities—how quickly do you need to know?"
"Great, I'm glad you're already using AlertMedia for communications. The question is—how are you currently detecting threats to send those alerts? That's where we come in. We integrate WITH AlertMedia to trigger the notifications automatically."

5 Category-Specific Strategies

Different competitor types require different approaches. Here's how to adjust your tactics.

vs Aggregators (Everbridge, Dataminr, Samdesk, Crisis24)

Lead With

  • Speed: "90 seconds vs 10-20 minutes—preventing vs responding"
  • Data moat: "We CREATE proprietary data (20M users + 911), they aggregate public sources"
  • Hyperlocal: "Block-level precision vs city-wide macro events"
  • Verification: "Ground truth from 911/CAD vs social media signals"

Key Discovery Questions

  • How critical is real-time detection vs 15-20 minute lag for your use case?
  • Do you need hyperlocal (block-level) or city-wide awareness?
  • Are you satisfied with aggregated public sources or need proprietary data?
  • What's your current alert speed for incidents near your facilities?

vs Communication Tools (AlertMedia, Rave)

Position as Complementary

  • "They're communication, we're detection—different problems"
  • "We integrate WITH them—Citizen alerts trigger AlertMedia workflows"
  • "You still need both: detection (us) + communication (them)"
  • "They send messages. We tell you WHAT to send messages about."

Key Discovery Questions

  • How are you currently monitoring threats to trigger your AlertMedia notifications?
  • Who decides when to send an alert, and what sources do they monitor?
  • How long does it take from incident happening to you knowing about it?
  • Are you monitoring off-campus/off-facility threats approaching your locations?
"Love that you're using Rave for on-campus emergencies. But what about threats APPROACHING campus? We're the early warning system for the area around your facilities. Layer us together."

vs Hybrid Competitors

Identify Their Primary Value Prop

  • Are they primarily aggregators with some communication features? → Use aggregator strategy
  • Are they primarily communication with basic threat feeds? → Use communication strategy
  • Ask: "What do you use [competitor] for most—detection or notification?"
  • Position around their weakness, not their claimed strength

6 Discovery Question Playbook

How to use battlecard questions strategically to uncover needs and position your solution.

Strategic Timing

  • Ask early: Questions help you understand their evaluation criteria before pitching
  • Listen actively: Their answers reveal pain points that map to your strengths
  • Take notes: Use their language in your pitch ("You mentioned 15-minute lag was a problem...")
  • Customize: Adjust your demo/proposal based on what you learn

Universal Questions (Ask on Every Call)

  • Walk me through what happens today when there's an incident near one of your facilities
  • How quickly do you need to know about threats? Minutes? Seconds?
  • What sources are you currently monitoring for situational awareness?
  • Who's responsible for watching those sources and making decisions?
  • What's the process from incident occurring to your team being notified?

How to Use Their Answers

  • "We monitor news feeds and social media" → "So you're aggregating public sources. What if we could create proprietary data for you?"
  • "Takes 10-15 minutes usually" → "What if you knew in 90 seconds? How would that change your response?"
  • "We have someone watch Twitter" → "What happens when that person is in a meeting or after hours?"
  • "We use [competitor]" → Pull up that battlecard and reference the discovery questions
"The best salespeople use questions to co-create the problem. Don't pitch Citizen. Ask questions that make THEM realize they need faster, more verified data. Then show them how we deliver it."

7 Common Objections & Responses

Battle-tested responses to the objections you'll hear most often. Use these verbatim.

"We're already using [competitor]"
"That's great! Are you satisfied with the speed and coverage? I ask because many customers use us WITH [competitor]—we handle hyperlocal real-time detection, they handle [communication/global events]. They're complementary, not competitive. Would you be open to seeing how we integrate together?"
"They offered us a free trial with gift cards for participation"
"We're happy to do a trial as well. What matters though is the data quality and speed when you actually need it in an emergency. Let me ask—when you tested them, how did they perform on detection speed for hyperlocal incidents? We average 90 seconds. What were you seeing from them?"
"You're more expensive than AlertMedia/Rave"
"Different solutions for different problems. They're communication platforms—sending messages. We're detection and situational awareness—knowing WHAT to send messages about. Most customers use both: Citizen for detection, AlertMedia for communication. We actually integrate together. Are you currently monitoring threats to trigger your AlertMedia alerts?"
"Everbridge/Dataminr is an enterprise standard"
"They're excellent enterprise platforms, no question. The question is whether you need proprietary data creation or aggregated feeds. They aggregate Twitter and news—data that's publicly available, just faster. We create proprietary data through 20M users and 911 integration. For hyperlocal incidents, we're 10-20 minutes faster because we're not waiting for someone to tweet about it. What's more valuable for your use case?"
"We need global coverage, you're US-only"
"Correct, we're focused on the US market where we have our proprietary data network. For global coverage, Crisis24 or Dataminr are great options. Many customers use us for US facilities (where we're faster and more accurate) and Crisis24 for international travel. Are most of your critical facilities domestic or international?"
"We don't have budget for another tool"
"I understand. Let me ask—what's the cost of NOT knowing about an incident for 15-20 minutes? One prevented evacuation, one avoided liability issue, one protected employee—what's that worth? We're $20K annually. If you're already spending $50K+ on Everbridge, we're a fraction of that cost for significantly faster detection. Want to walk through an ROI scenario specific to your facilities?"

Pro Tip: The Pause

After answering an objection, PAUSE. Let them respond. Don't over-explain or get defensive. Confident silence shows you believe in your answer.

10 Feedback Loop

Competitive intelligence is only valuable if it stays current. Here's how you help keep these battlecards sharp.

Share New Objections

  • If you hear a new objection not covered in the battlecards, share it in #b2bsalesteam immediately
  • Include: the competitor, the objection verbatim, and how you responded
  • Sales ops will test responses and add winners to the battlecards
  • This creates a compounding knowledge advantage for the whole team

Flag Competitor Changes

  • New pricing you hear about? Share it.
  • Competitor changed positioning or launched new features? Flag it.
  • Lost a deal to a competitor for a new reason? Document it.
  • Heard a prospect mention competitor weakness you haven't seen before? Share it.

Win/Loss Reasons

  • When you WIN against a competitor: What was the deciding factor? Which talk track sealed it?
  • When you LOSE to a competitor: What did they do better? What objection couldn't you overcome?
  • This intel directly informs battlecard updates and new positioning strategies
  • Quarterly win/loss reviews help identify patterns across the team

How to Submit Feedback

  • Slack: Post in #b2bsalesteam with tag @sales-ops
  • Template: "Battlecard feedback - [Competitor]: [what you learned] | [context/source]"
  • Weekly: Sales ops reviews all feedback and updates battlecards monthly
  • Recognition: Top contributors get highlighted in team meetings and influence strategy

The Compounding Advantage

Every deal you run teaches you something new. Every competitor interaction reveals their strategy. When you share that knowledge, the entire team gets smarter.

Your experience + team's experience + continuous updates = unfair competitive advantage

"Competitive intelligence depreciates daily. The battlecards from 6 months ago are worth 50% less if they haven't been updated. Your field reports are what keep them sharp. Share what you learn."
